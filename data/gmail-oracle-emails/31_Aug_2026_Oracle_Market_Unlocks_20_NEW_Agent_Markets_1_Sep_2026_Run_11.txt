## 1. Railway Template Marketplace / OSS Partner Program — 24/25

**What agents can sell/perform.** Production-ready deployable stacks: agent gateways, MCP servers, vector/search stacks, inference gateways, webhook workers, observability bundles, self-hosted SaaS, databases plus dashboards, vertical automation backends and hardened open-source services. The product is not a ZIP; it is a deployment recipe that continuously earns from real infrastructure usage.

**Autonomous workflow.** Oracle mines GitHub/Reddit/Railway request patterns and template gaps → Worker selects a maintained OSS project or composes a multi-service stack → generates Railway template variables, health checks, storage, domains and docs → `CloudWorld` deploys disposable copies and tests cold start, persistence, upgrades, secrets, failure recovery and cost → publish template/partner listing → users deploy → Railway usage produces recurring commission → usage/support telemetry feeds Worker → upstream releases trigger regression tests and automatic template patches.

**Human dependency.** H1: partner/payout identity and any initial approval. After that, runtime and maintenance can approach H0.

**Infrastructure/unlock.** Railway’s OSS Partner Program exposes deployment templates as economic assets. Railway says creators can earn **25% commission on deployments** (15% usage + 10% tied to support/community participation depending on program terms), with template metrics and verified marketplace visibility.

**Demand evidence.** Railway reported in December 2025 that it had paid **about $1M** to developers who built templates, including roughly $730k in cash; two contributors had earned over $100k, six over $10k, and thirty over $1k. Railway also advertises 1,800+ templates and roughly 2M developers. This is unusually concrete proof that a small deployable artifact can produce recurring infrastructure revenue.

**Community pain.** General 2026 integration discussions continue to report OAuth refresh failures, rate-limit surprises, dropped webhooks, schema drift and bad sandboxes. That makes maintained templates materially more valuable than one-shot generated deploy buttons.

**Why agents fit.** Deployment artifacts are bounded, declarative, regression-testable and maintenance-heavy. A persistent specialist can own upstream-version drift better than a human who ships once and forgets it.

**Moltwork assets.** `RailwayPack`, `CloudWorld`, deployment-cost evaluator, upgrade/failure-recovery evaluator, `TemplateSchool`, upstream-changelog Skill, persistent `RailwayWorker`.

**Economics.** Attractive when a template has recurring compute/storage usage and low support. Unattractive when the underlying OSS deploy is trivial, zero-usage, or users churn after one experiment.

Sources: https://blog.railway.com/p/1-million-paid-to-developers-who-built-railway-templates ; https://railway.com/partners

## 2. Tiendanube / Nuvemshop App Store — AI & Agents — 24/25

**What agents can sell.** Merchant micro-SaaS and agent capabilities for LATAM ecommerce: catalog QA, WhatsApp/order operations, B2B ordering, margin/inventory alerts, product-data cleanup, tax/shipping adapters, returns, seller analytics, merchandising and MCP/Skill-based merchant actions.

**Workflow.** Mine Portuguese/Spanish merchant pain + App Store gaps → specialist generates OAuth app using Tiendanube APIs/NubeSDK → `CommerceWorld` seeds products, orders, customers, coupons and fulfillment states → Harbor checks side effects, idempotence, permissions and language/locale correctness → app homologation → App Store publication and pricing → merchant installs → recurring fee/usage → webhooks + uninstall data feed Worker → regression against API/NubeSDK changes.

**H-level.** H1–H2 because public listing/homologation and commercial identity remain human; operation can be H0 after merchant OAuth.

**Frontier infrastructure.** The developer portal now has an explicit **“AI & Agents”** path for **MCPs, Skills and Connectors**, alongside APIs/SDKs and the App Store. The company says the ecosystem reaches **180,000+ active stores** and 600+ partners. Apps can charge monthly or one-time fees, with the platform supporting commercial distribution.

**2026 unlock.** New apps have had to use **NubeSDK since June 5, 2026**, while older script-based apps are being progressively removed by **October 30, 2026**. That creates migration/compatibility demand right now.

**Demand/community.** LATAM ecommerce is operationally messy: WhatsApp, local payment/shipping methods, multilingual catalogs and merchant back-office work create narrow gaps that global Shopify-first vendors often ignore. Reddit signal is thinner than official ecosystem evidence, so treat merchant forums/App Store reviews as the stronger source rather than inventing Reddit demand.

**Why agents fit.** Commerce workflows are structured and event-driven; many failures have machine-checkable outcomes.

**Moltwork assets.** `NuvemshopPack`, LATAM `CommerceWorld`, Portuguese/Spanish Merchant School, NubeSDK migration Skill, catalog/order evaluator, partner-submission Process.

**Economics.** Attractive if the same Worker serves many merchants with little onboarding. Unattractive if local tax/support obligations turn every account into bespoke consulting.

Sources: https://dev.nuvemshop.com.br/ ; official app-install/homologation docs in the Tiendanube/Nuvemshop developer portal.

## 3. Aircall App Marketplace — 23/25

**What agents can sell.** Contact-center workflow products: call qualification, CRM enrichment, compliance evidence, after-call structured extraction, appointment/dispatch actions, vertical call routing, QA, number/user administration, outbound-agent tooling and specialist actions callable by AI voice agents.

**Workflow.** Discover repeated call-ops pain → create OAuth marketplace app → `CallCenterWorld` replays synthetic calls/users/queues/CRM states → verify transcription/action mapping, permissions, retries and high-risk side effects → marketplace review → install → external recurring billing → ingest call/events/analytics → Worker improves policies and keeps API compatibility.

**H-level.** H1–H2: partner/listing review plus customer OAuth. Runtime can be H0 for read/assist actions; consequential outbound actions should retain approvals.

**Frontier unlock.** Aircall’s current developer site explicitly promotes **outbound AI agent calls** and the ability to **give agents actions through MCP**. It also added WhatsApp API and analytics features in June 2026 and outbound AI-agent calls in May 2026. Aircall says marketplace developers can reach **20,000+ businesses**.

**Time-sensitive demand.** Aircall User API V1/webhook consumers face a **September 30, 2026** migration deadline, including changes to user-availability semantics. A compatibility/migration Worker can monetize a temporary but concrete pain spike.

**Community constraints.** Contact-center integrations fail on hidden state, user availability, webhooks, authentication and CRM drift—the kind of repetitive edge cases a persistent Worker can accumulate.

**Moltwork assets.** `AircallPack`, `CallCenterWorld`, call-action evaluator, MCP action Skill, V1→V2 migration Process, `VoiceOpsSchool`.

**Economics.** Attractive when an action saves measurable agent minutes or prevents missed calls/revenue; unattractive when the product is merely generic summarization already bundled by Aircall.

Sources: https://developer.aircall.io/ ; Aircall App Marketplace docs.

## 4. Paddle Apps / Marketplace Publisher Program — 23/25

**What agents can sell.** Billing/revenue-stack apps for Paddle merchants: entitlement reconciliation, subscription migration, cancellation/recovery workflows, revenue analytics, tax/accounting exports, pricing experiments, product provisioning, webhook reliability and vertical SaaS operations.

**Workflow.** Mine SaaS billing pain → build scoped OAuth app → `BillingWorld` simulates customers, subscriptions, upgrades, retries, refunds and entitlements → monetary-invariant evaluator → apply to publisher program → app listing/installation → usage/external pricing or app commercial model → observe merchant events/errors → Worker patches automatically.

**H-level.** H1 once publisher/KYC/review is complete. High-impact billing mutations should retain explicit merchant authorization.

**Major 2026 unlock.** On **June 26, 2026**, Paddle released **OAuth apps** and announced it was **opening an app marketplace publisher program**, with a marketplace for merchants to discover/install apps “coming soon.” Existing integrations can migrate toward scoped OAuth instead of bespoke credential handling.

**Demand evidence.** This is a timing bet rather than a mature revenue market. The platform-side unlock is strong; independent marketplace GMV evidence does not yet exist. 2026 SaaS developers still complain broadly about OAuth refresh/token lifecycle and billing-integration complexity, so a good specialist can arrive before the catalog saturates.

**Why agents fit.** Billing products are deterministic enough to test: invoice totals, entitlements, proration, webhook state and reconciliation all have hard invariants.

**Moltwork assets.** `PaddlePack`, `BillingWorld`, monetary evaluator, OAuth lifecycle Skill, MarketplaceSubmissionProcess, `RevenueOpsWorker`.

**Economics.** Attractive because being early can compound distribution and review history. Unattractive if the marketplace launch is delayed or native Paddle absorbs the obvious utilities.

Sources: https://developer.paddle.com/changelog/2026/oauth-apps ; https://developer.paddle.com/build/apps

**Moltwork assets.** `PointClickCarePack`, PHI-safe AdminWorld, audit/evidence evaluator, HealthcareAdminSchool, partner-cert Process, compliance guardrails.

**Economics.** Attractive for narrow high-dollar administrative tasks. Unattractive if clinical liability, procurement time or customer-specific implementation dominates.

**Why agents fit.** This is nearly the ideal callable-asset loop: machine makes product, machine evaluates product, machine buys product.

**Moltwork assets.** generic `APIPack`, `APIWorld`, latency/schema evaluator, endpoint factory Skill, pricing experiment Process.

**Economics.** Attractive if buyer traffic exists and capability has proprietary data/process; unattractive if it is just a trivial wrapper another model can recreate in minutes.

# Frontier Unlocks

### 1. Paddle: OAuth turned integration software into a marketplace-ready object
The **June 26, 2026** OAuth Apps launch is exactly the sort of event Oracle should score as an H-level decrease. Old state: merchant supplies/adapts credentials. New state: scoped OAuth install, connected-app management, publisher program, forthcoming discovery marketplace.

### 2. Tiendanube/Nuvemshop: “AI & Agents” is now a first-class developer category
This is stronger than a generic REST API. The platform is explicitly organizing **MCPs, Skills and Connectors** as developer artifacts, against a real merchant base, while NubeSDK creates a standardized app runtime/approval path.

### 3. Aircall: agents can call actions through MCP inside a commercial communications ecosystem
The economically relevant unit becomes the action—e.g. `BookAppointment`, `ValidateWarranty`, `UpdateCRM`, `EscalateClaim`—rather than “an AI chatbot.” These can be evaluated independently and reused by many higher-level agents.

### 4. Railway: deployment templates are already recurring-revenue assets
This is important because it proves an autonomous agent does not need to sell labor. It can manufacture an **infrastructure object** that other machines/users repeatedly instantiate and that meters revenue as the deployment runs.

### 5. Browser/WebMCP tooling is reducing the residual form-filling H-layer
Emerging WebMCP-compatible tooling is beginning to turn ordinary web forms into structured agent actions. An Auto WebMCP extension updated **August 30, 2026** demonstrates converting standard HTML forms into tools exposed through `document.modelContext` on newer Chrome builds. Treat this as frontier tooling rather than authoritative proof that every marketplace supports WebMCP today—but it shows how listing/submission surfaces that currently require browser automation can become structured.

### 6. The callable-asset pattern keeps expanding
Railway templates are deployable assets; Tiendanube Skills/MCPs and Aircall MCP actions are callable assets; api0 APIs are directly metered callable assets. Oracle should apply a **Callable Asset Multiplier** when the thing being sold can itself be invoked by another agent with minimal human handling.

# Human Dependency Reduction

The most economically valuable automation target is no longer raw code generation. It is compressing **H2 marketplace entry into one H1 accountable approval**.

## A. PartnerGateCompiler

Input: a validated product. Output:

- OAuth scopes / least-privilege analysis
- architecture diagram
- data-flow/privacy map
- security/threat model
- sandbox test evidence
- compatibility matrix
- reviewer credentials/demo fixture
- screenshots/video/listing copy
- pricing/support/SLA answers
- provenance/IP record
- release notes/changelog
- partner questionnaire drafts

HumanQueue then handles only identity, legal attestations, signatures, subjective representations and genuine relationship approval.

This single Process is reusable across Aircall, PointClickCare, Mindbody, Simpro, Cin7, Constant Contact, Zuora and most enterprise venues already discovered.

## B. VenueWorldFactory

The bigger compounding asset is a family of deterministic Worlds rather than one bespoke harness per marketplace:

**CloudWorld** → Railway + DigitalOcean/Heroku/Vercel-type prior venues  
**CommerceWorld** → Tiendanube + Jumpseller + prior Shopify/SHOPLINE/etc.  
**BillingWorld** → Paddle + Zuora + prior Chargebee  
**CallCenterWorld** → Aircall + Dialpad + prior Front/Gorgias/Crisp/Talkdesk/Genesys  
**FieldServiceWorld** → Simpro + prior ServiceTitan/Housecall Pro  
**AccountingWorld** → FreshBooks + prior Fortnox/Visma/Exact/Xero  
**WellnessWorld** → Mindbody  
**NASWorld** → Synology  
**VirtualWorldWorld** → Second Life / IMVU / Core

Every new Venue then becomes mostly an adapter and a handful of venue-specific invariants.

## C. OAuth/credential handoff should become a one-action HumanQueue

`Worker requests scope → compiler generates least-privilege rationale → human approves consent once → callback credential self-test → Worker resumes automatically.`

Do not try to remove legitimate consent. Remove everything around it.

## D. Marketplace-submission browser steps are the next WebMCP target

Many remaining H1/H2 steps are not cognitively human; they are just unstructured forms, screenshots, file uploads and review dashboards. WebMCP/structured browser tooling can reduce this dramatically. Prioritize surfaces that repeat across every release: metadata, changelogs, screenshots, scopes, pricing, test credentials and reviewer instructions.

## E. Persistent maintenance is the real specialist moat

General integration pain in 2026 remains remarkably stable: OAuth refresh tokens expire unexpectedly, rate limits differ from docs, webhooks drop, schemas change and sandboxes fail to match production. That means the valuable Moltwork worker is not “agent that can build a connector.” It is:

`specialist owns product forever → watches upstream → detects break → reproduces → patches → Harbor regression → releases → accumulates failure memory.`

After enough jobs, two identical base-model agents cease to be economically identical because one owns a domain’s edge-case history, evaluators, fixtures and proven Processes.

Frontier Unlocks

1. Paddle: OAuth turned integration software into a marketplace-ready object
<p>The <strong>June 26, 2026</strong> OAuth Apps launch is exactly the sort of event Oracle should score as an H-level decrease. Old state: merchant supplies/adapts credentials. New state: scoped OAuth install, connected-app management, publisher program, forthcoming discovery marketplace.</p>

2. Tiendanube/Nuvemshop: “AI &amp; Agents” is now a first-class developer category
<p>This is stronger than a generic REST API. The platform is explicitly organizing <strong>MCPs, Skills and Connectors</strong> as developer artifacts, against a real merchant base, while NubeSDK creates a standardized app runtime/approval path.</p>

3. Aircall: agents can call actions through MCP inside a commercial communications ecosystem
<p>The economically relevant unit becomes the action—e.g. <code>BookAppointment</code>, <code>ValidateWarranty</code>, <code>UpdateCRM</code>, <code>EscalateClaim</code>—rather than “an AI chatbot.” These can be evaluated independently and reused by many higher-level agents.</p>

4. Railway: deployment templates are already recurring-revenue assets
<p>This is important because it proves an autonomous agent does not need to sell labor. It can manufacture an <strong>infrastructure object</strong> that other machines/users repeatedly instantiate and that meters revenue as the deployment runs.</p>

5. Browser/WebMCP tooling is reducing the residual form-filling H-layer
<p>Emerging WebMCP-compatible tooling is beginning to turn ordinary web forms into structured agent actions. An Auto WebMCP extension updated <strong>August 30, 2026</strong> demonstrates converting standard HTML forms into tools exposed through <code>document.modelContext</code> on newer Chrome builds. Treat this as frontier tooling rather than authoritative proof that every marketplace supports WebMCP today—but it shows how listing/submission surfaces that currently require browser automation can become structured.</p>

6. The callable-asset pattern keeps expanding
<p>Railway templates are deployable assets; Tiendanube Skills/MCPs and Aircall MCP actions are callable assets; api0 APIs are directly metered callable assets. Oracle should apply a <strong>Callable Asset Multiplier</strong> when the thing being sold can itself be invoked by another agent with minimal human handling.</p>



Human Dependency Reduction
<p>The most economically valuable automation target is no longer raw code generation. It is compressing <strong>H2 marketplace entry into one H1 accountable approval</strong>.</p>

A. PartnerGateCompiler
<p>Input: a validated product. Output:</p>
<ul>
<li>OAuth scopes / least-privilege analysis</li>
<li>architecture diagram</li>
<li>data-flow/privacy map</li>
<li>security/threat model</li>
<li>sandbox test evidence</li>
<li>compatibility matrix</li>
<li>reviewer credentials/demo fixture</li>
<li>screenshots/video/listing copy</li>
<li>pricing/support/SLA answers</li>
<li>provenance/IP record</li>
<li>release notes/changelog</li>
<li>partner questionnaire drafts</li>
</ul>
<p>HumanQueue then handles only identity, legal attestations, signatures, subjective representations and genuine relationship approval.</p>
<p>This single Process is reusable across Aircall, PointClickCare, Mindbody, Simpro, Cin7, Constant Contact, Zuora and most enterprise venues already discovered.</p>

B. VenueWorldFactory
<p>The bigger compounding asset is a family of deterministic Worlds rather than one bespoke harness per marketplace:</p>
<p><strong>CloudWorld</strong> → Railway + DigitalOcean/Heroku/Vercel-type prior venues<br>
<strong>CommerceWorld</strong> → Tiendanube + Jumpseller + prior Shopify/SHOPLINE/etc.<br>
<strong>BillingWorld</strong> → Paddle + Zuora + prior Chargebee<br>
<strong>CallCenterWorld</strong> → Aircall + Dialpad + prior Front/Gorgias/Crisp/Talkdesk/Genesys<br>
<strong>FieldServiceWorld</strong> → Simpro + prior ServiceTitan/Housecall Pro<br>
<strong>AccountingWorld</strong> → FreshBooks + prior Fortnox/Visma/Exact/Xero<br>
<strong>WellnessWorld</strong> → Mindbody<br>
<strong>NASWorld</strong> → Synology<br>
<strong>VirtualWorldWorld</strong> → Second Life / IMVU / Core</p>
<p>Every new Venue then becomes mostly an adapter and a handful of venue-specific invariants.</p>

C. OAuth/credential handoff should become a one-action HumanQueue
<p><code>Worker requests scope → compiler generates least-privilege rationale → human approves consent once → callback credential self-test → Worker resumes automatically.</code></p>
<p>Do not try to remove legitimate consent. Remove everything around it.</p>

D. Marketplace-submission browser steps are the next WebMCP target
<p>Many remaining H1/H2 steps are not cognitively human; they are just unstructured forms, screenshots, file uploads and review dashboards. WebMCP/structured browser tooling can reduce this dramatically. Prioritize surfaces that repeat across every release: metadata, changelogs, screenshots, scopes, pricing, test credentials and reviewer instructions.</p>

E. Persistent maintenance is the real specialist moat
<p>General integration pain in 2026 remains remarkably stable: OAuth refresh tokens expire unexpectedly, rate limits differ from docs, webhooks drop, schemas change and sandboxes fail to match production. That means the valuable Moltwork worker is not “agent that can build a connector.” It is:</p>
<p><code>specialist owns product forever → watches upstream → detects break → reproduces → patches → Harbor regression → releases → accumulates failure memory.</code></p>
<p>After enough jobs, two identical base-model agents cease to be economically identical because one owns a domain’s edge-case history, evaluators, fixtures and proven Processes.</p>



Best experiments from this batch
<ol>
<li><strong>Railway</strong> — build one useful agent/MCP infrastructure template and test whether autonomous maintenance can generate real recurring deployment revenue. This has the best proven payout evidence.</li>
<li><strong>Tiendanube/Nuvemshop</strong> — ship a narrow LATAM merchant Action/Skill while the AI &amp; Agents category and NubeSDK transition are fresh.</li>
<li><strong>Aircall</strong> — build one deterministic action consumed by a voice agent rather than another generic voice agent; exploit the September 30 V1 migration window.</li>
<li><strong>Paddle</strong> — join the publisher program early and build a billing/reconciliation app before the marketplace catalog fills.</li>
<li><strong>api0.app</strong> — deliberately use this as a <em>market validation experiment</em>: can a Moltwork Worker create a callable API asset, publish it, and receive a payment with almost no human work? Low independent demand is the risk, but the experiment is cheap.</li>
</ol>
<p>The strongest cross-run conclusion is that Oracle should now score <strong>Economic Callability × Portability × Persistence</strong> rather than raw marketplace TAM. A modest venue can be more valuable than a huge one when its artifact is machine-callable, its World/Worker ports to adjacent venues, and every real job makes the specialist measurably better.</p>
